How does Tokio's spawning model differ from smol's?

Both run futures as lightweight tasks, but they draw the line in different places. Tokio ships a multi-threaded, work-stealing scheduler with its own I/O driver and timers, and tokio::spawn needs futures that are Send + 'static

smol stays small: it composes separate crates for the executor and I/O, and leaves the threading model to you

  • Tokio — batteries included; most libraries expect it
  • smol — fewer moving parts, easy to read end to end
