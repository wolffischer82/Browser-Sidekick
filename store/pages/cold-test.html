Do heat pumps work in a real cold snap?

We followed 40 homes through a winter that dropped to −22 °C. Most stayed warm; a few lessons stood out.

By Mira Lindqvist · 9 min read

Modern air-source heat pumps kept 37 of the 40 homes at their set temperature on every day of the test, including a week below −15 °C. The three that struggled were older houses whose pumps had been sized for an average winter, not the coldest one.

Efficiency falls as it gets colder. Across the test, the average seasonal coefficient of performance (COP) was 3.1, but on the coldest days it dropped to around 1.8 — still nearly twice as efficient as direct electric heating.

Installers we spoke to were clear: the single most important decision is sizing. A pump chosen for the design temperature of the region, rather than a mild average, rarely needs its backup heater.

Noise and defrost cycles were the most common complaints, but only four households said they noticed either after the first month.
