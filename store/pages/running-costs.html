Heat pump or gas boiler: what does a year of heating cost?

We ran the numbers for a typical 120 m² house at today's energy prices.

By Jonas Weber · 6 min read

For a well-insulated 120 m² house needing 12,000 kWh of heat a year, a heat pump with a seasonal COP of 3.0 uses about 4,000 kWh of electricity. At 0.32 per kWh, that is roughly 1,280 a year.

The same house heated by a 90 %-efficient gas boiler burns about 13,300 kWh of gas. At 0.11 per kWh plus the standing charge, the bill comes to around 1,560 a year.

The gap widens with a heat-pump tariff or rooftop solar, and narrows in poorly insulated homes where the pump has to run hotter.

Upfront cost remains the biggest hurdle: installed prices start around 11,000 before grants, against about 4,000 for a new boiler.
